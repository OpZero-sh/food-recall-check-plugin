# Reading a food label for a recall check

What to ask the user for and where each item is usually printed. Ask only for what the record needs.

## The identifiers

| Item | What it is | Where it usually is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand and product name | As printed on the front, including variety or flavor | Front panel |
| Net quantity | Weight, volume or count, such as "8 oz (226 g)" | Bottom of the front panel |
| UPC | The 12 digits under the barcode. Include the small digits at each end | Back or bottom of the package |
| Lot or batch code | A short run of letters and digits identifying the production run. It may be labeled LOT, L, BATCH, or have no label at all | Stamped, inkjet printed or embossed, often near the date. Common spots: the bottom of a can or jar, the shoulder or cap of a bottle, the end flap of a carton, the back seam or top seal of a bag, the rim of a tub or lid |
| Best by, use by or sell by date | The date the manufacturer prints for quality or freshness | Usually next to the lot code |
| Manufacturer or distributor | "Manufactured by", "Distributed by" or "Packed for", with a city and state | Small print on the back or side panel |

## Tips to pass on

- The lot code is often printed in a different ink or stamped into the material. It is not part of the barcode.
- Read codes exactly as printed, with every letter, digit, space and symbol.
- If the code is smudged or cut off, say so. A partial code cannot confirm or rule out a match.
- A photo is fine if the user's client supports images, but confirm any character that is hard to read.
- Store brand products name the store as distributor. The recalling firm in the record may be the manufacturer, whose name is not on the package.
- The date a product was bought and the store's name and location help when a record lists retailers or states, but they do not decide a match.

## Signs the product is outside FDA food recall data

- A USDA mark of inspection, a round or shield shaped mark with an establishment number such as "EST. 1234" or "P-1234", means the product was inspected by USDA. Meat, poultry and certain egg products are recalled through USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service, listed at https://www.fsis.usda.gov/recalls.
- Food prepared and sold by a restaurant, deli counter or market stall may be handled by state or local health authorities.
- Products bought outside the United States are covered by that country's food safety authority.

## Privacy

Do not ask for receipts, loyalty card numbers, addresses or payment details. None of them are needed for a recall check.
